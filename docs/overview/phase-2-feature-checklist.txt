THE 30-SECOND VERSION
================================================================================

Phase 1: photograph an object, get a 3D model.
Phase 2: turn those models into a public storefront with a permanent QR code.

    photograph  ->  pick the model  ->  build the catalog  ->  preview
                                                                  |
                            analytics  <-  QR code  <-  PUBLISH  <-+

Four rules the whole design rests on:

    1. Nothing is live until Publish is pressed.
    2. The QR code never changes.
    3. A half-finished publish says exactly which half.
    4. No technical error ever reaches the screen.

================================================================================
THE FOUR THINGS TO SAY IN A DEMO
================================================================================

  1. "A capture is not a product."
     One capture can produce several 3D models. The user sees all of them and
     picks the one that actually looks right. Most tools hide this and pick
     for you.

  2. "Nothing is live until you press Publish."
     Edit a live catalog all day. Customers keep seeing the last published
     snapshot. There is no way to break the public page mid-edit.

  3. "The QR code is permanent."
     Print it on a standee on day one. Change every price, rename the
     business, republish fifty times, take the catalog offline and bring it
     back. The sticker keeps working.

  4. "A half-finished publish tells you which half."
     Ten products, two fail, you see exactly which two and why, and retry
     pushes those two only.


================================================================================
THE SIX THINGS NOT TO PROMISE IN A DEMO
================================================================================

Each is a limit of the public catalog platform, not of the app.

  1. Out-of-stock does not show to customers. It filters the owner's list.
  2. Tags are not visible to customers.
  3. Website and social links do not appear on the public page.
  4. Drag order inside the app does not fully carry to the public page.
  5. iPhone AR room placement is not served yet; iPhone customers get the 3D
     viewer instead.
  6. Converting a photo-only product to 3D restarts that product's analytics
     history.


================================================================================
NEXT: SAME-DAY REP ACTIVATION -- DESIGNED, NOT BUILT
================================================================================

Today the business owner does every step themselves. The next phase adds a
SALES REP who walks in with a box of pre-printed QR standees, does the whole
job on the owner's behalf in one visit, sticks a standee on the table, and
leaves with the catalog live.

From the owner's side the entire journey collapses into: "someone came, took
photos of my food, and left me a QR code that works."

Two genuinely new mechanisms make it possible:

[ ] Pre-printed QR codes.
    Standees are printed BEFORE anyone knows which restaurant gets them. An
    unassigned scan shows a "not live yet" page. Activation binds the code to
    a catalog on the spot. This is why the QR must resolve through a redirect
    rather than pointing straight at the public page.

[ ] Publishing before the models exist.
    A dish goes live as a photo the same afternoon and quietly becomes a 3D
    product hours later when generation finishes -- on the SAME public entry,
    so it keeps the analytics history it built up as a photo.

Seven build stages, none started:

    [ ] 1  Foundations -- rep role, config, client role fix
    [ ] 2  QR inventory + admin batch minting
    [ ] 3  Public resolver for scanned codes
    [ ] 4  Delegation + rep activation
    [ ] 5  Pending models + asset promotion
    [ ] 6  The rep app
    [ ] 7  Verification + rollout
